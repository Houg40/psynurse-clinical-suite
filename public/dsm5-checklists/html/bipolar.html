Bipolar I Disorder (Mania, Hypomania & Depression) F31.x (Bipolar I) / F31.81 (Bipolar II)

Bipolar I requires at least one lifetime Manic Episode (lasting >= 1 week or requiring hospitalization). Hypomania requires >= 4 consecutive days without marked impairment. Bipolar depression involves a Major Depressive Episode.

Criterion A: Distinct Mood Episode Distinct period of persistently elevated, expansive, or irritable mood + increased activity/energy
A_Mania: MANIA: Lasting at least 1 week and present most of the day, nearly every day (or any duration if hospitalization is required).
A_Hypomania: HYPOMANIA: Lasting at least 4 consecutive days, present most of the day, nearly every day.
Criterion B: Symptom Checklist (Requires >= 3 symptoms, or >= 4 if mood is only irritable) Represents a noticeable change from baseline
B1: Inflated self-esteem or grandiosity (grandiosity may include delusions of invincibility, superiority, special abilities, fortune, or messianic beliefs).
B2: Decreased need for sleep (e.g., feels fully rested after only 3 hours of sleep).
B3: More talkative than usual or pressure to keep talking (rapid, loud, interruptive speech).
B4: Flight of ideas or subjective experience that thoughts are racing.
B5: Distractibility (attention too easily drawn to unimportant or irrelevant external stimuli).
B6: Increase in goal-directed activity (socially, at work/school, or sexually) or psychomotor agitation (purposeless, non-goal-directed activity).
B7: Excessive involvement in activities that have a high potential for painful consequences (e.g., unrestrained buying sprees, sexual indiscretions, foolish business investments).
Criterion C & D: Severity, Hospitalization & Psychosis Distinguishes Mania from Hypomania
Mania_Severity: MANIA (Criterion C): Severe enough to cause marked impairment in social/occupational functioning, or necessitates hospitalization, or features psychotic symptoms (grandiose/persecutory delusions).
Hypomania_Severity: HYPOMANIA (Criterion E): Not severe enough to cause marked impairment or hospitalization, and ABSOLUTELY NO psychotic features (presence of psychosis = automatic Mania).
