Attention-Deficit / Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) F90.2 (Combined) / F90.0 (Inattentive) / F90.1 (Hyperactive-Impulsive)

A persistent pattern of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity that interferes with functioning or development. For older adolescents and adults (age 17+), at least 5 symptoms are required per domain; for children, at least 6 are required. Symptoms must persist for at least 6 months.

Criterion A1: Inattention Requires >= 5 symptoms for adults (age 17+) or >= 6 for children for at least 6 months
A1a: Often fails to give close attention to details or makes careless mistakes in schoolwork, at work, or during other activities (overlooks/misses details, work is inaccurate).
A1b: Often has difficulty sustaining attention in tasks or play activities (difficulty remaining focused during lectures, conversations, or lengthy reading).
A1c: Often does not seem to listen when spoken to directly (mind seems elsewhere, even in the absence of any obvious distraction).
A1d: Often does not follow through on instructions and fails to finish schoolwork, chores, or duties in the workplace (starts tasks but quickly loses focus and is easily sidetracked).
A1e: Often has difficulty organizing tasks and activities (difficulty managing sequential tasks; messy, disorganized work; poor time management; fails to meet deadlines).
A1f: Often avoids, dislikes, or is reluctant to engage in tasks that require sustained mental effort (e.g., preparing reports, completing forms, reviewing lengthy papers).
A1g: Often loses things necessary for tasks or activities (school materials, tools, wallets, keys, paperwork, eyeglasses, mobile telephone).
A1h: Is often easily distracted by extraneous stimuli (for older adolescents and adults, may include unrelated thoughts).
A1i: Is often forgetful in daily activities (doing chores, running errands; for older adolescents and adults, returning calls, paying bills, keeping appointments).
Criterion A2: Hyperactivity & Impulsivity Requires >= 5 symptoms for adults (age 17+) or >= 6 for children for at least 6 months
A2a: Often fidgets with or taps hands or feet or squirms in seat.
A2b: Often leaves seat in situations when remaining seated is expected (leaves place in office/workplace, classroom, or meetings).
A2c: Often runs about or climbs in situations where it is inappropriate (in adolescents/adults, may be limited to feeling restless).
A2d: Often unable to play or engage in leisure activities quietly.
A2e: Is often 'on the go,' acting as if 'driven by a motor' (uncomfortable being still for extended time, as in restaurants or meetings; perceived by others as restless or hard to keep up with).
A2f: Often talks excessively.
A2g: Often blurts out an answer before a question has been completed (completes people's sentences; cannot wait for turn in conversation).
A2h: Often has difficulty waiting his or her turn (e.g., while waiting in line).
A2i: Often interrupts or intrudes on others (butts into conversations, games, or activities; may take over what others are doing).
Criteria B, C, D & E: Chronicity & Context All must be confirmed for formal diagnosis
B: Criterion B: Several inattentive or hyperactive-impulsive symptoms were present prior to age 12 years.
C: Criterion C: Several symptoms are present in two or more settings (e.g., home, work/school, with friends or relatives, in other activities).
D: Criterion D: Clear evidence that symptoms interfere with or reduce the quality of social, academic, or occupational functioning.
E: Criterion E: Symptoms do not occur exclusively during schizophrenia or another psychotic disorder and are not better explained by another mental disorder.
