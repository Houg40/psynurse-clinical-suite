Sedative, Hypnotic, or Anxiolytic Use Disorder F13.10 (Mild) / F13.20 (Moderate/Severe)

Problematic pattern of benzodiazepine, sedative, or hypnotic drug use leading to clinically significant impairment or distress within a 12-month period.

DSM-5 11 Criteria Checklist (Within 12-Month Period) Mild: 2–3 • Moderate: 4–5 • Severe: 6+
1: Sedatives/anxiolytics often taken in larger amounts or over a longer period than intended.
2: Persistent desire or unsuccessful efforts to reduce or control sedative/anxiolytic use.
3: Great deal of time spent trying to obtain, use, or recover from sedatives/anxiolytics.
4: Craving, or strong desire to use the sedative/anxiolytic.
5: Recurrent sedative use results in failure to fulfill major obligations at work, school, or home.
6: Continued use despite persistent social or interpersonal problems.
7: Important activities given up or reduced because of sedative/anxiolytic use.
8: Recurrent sedative use in situations that are physically hazardous.
9: Continued use despite knowledge of physical/psychological problem likely caused by drug.
10: Tolerance developed (not counted if taken solely under appropriate medical supervision).
11: Withdrawal syndrome (autonomic hyperactivity, hand tremor, insomnia, nausea/vomiting, transient hallucinations, psychomotor agitation, anxiety, grand mal seizures; or use to avoid symptoms).
